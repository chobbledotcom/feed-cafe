# Voice & Tone

Feed Cafe & Deli should sound like a friendly, healthy cafe in St Ives: warm, easy to understand, fresh without being smug, and practical enough for parents, swimmers, regulars and people just looking for lunch.

The voice should feel like someone at the cafe wrote it after a busy morning, not like a wellness brand, a tourist board, or a restaurant PR agency. It can be cheerful, but it should not grin at the reader.

This is a guide, not a script. Use it to keep the writing in the right place, then let the page do its job.

## The Trap To Avoid

Do not turn Feed into a lifestyle brand. The food can be fresh, colourful and healthy, but the copy should not make people feel like they are being sold a personal transformation with their coffee.

Avoid the other trap too: do not make it sound like a generic family cafe with "something for everyone" on every page. Feed has real cues to work with: breakfast until 11.30am, smoothies, power bowls, loaded fries, cakes, coffee, kids food, Stay and Play, the green panelling, the wood counter, the menu board, and the leisure centre setting.

The voice should come from those specifics.

## The WhatsApp Test

Read the line out loud. Could James or someone behind the counter send it to a customer in a message without sounding like they had asked a copywriter for help? If not, make it plainer.

This does not mean the copy should be sloppy. It should just feel natural, useful and kind.

## Source Feel

Do not copy a person or brand too closely. The shared feel we want is:

- A good independent cafe that knows its menu and its regulars.
- A parent-friendly place where the wording is clear and not precious.
- A health-conscious cafe that still serves brownies, burgers and loaded fries without apologising for them.
- A Cornish cafe that can mention St Ives without sounding like a holiday cottage brochure.
- A leisure centre cafe that is honest about being useful, busy and easy to drop into.

When stuck, look at what is actually in the room and on the board before inventing a mood.

## Principles

### 1. Friendly Before Clever

The writing should make people feel welcome and informed. It does not need jokes, slogans, or a crafted "brand voice" moment in every section.

Good:

- "Breakfast is served until 11.30am, with coffee, bagels, bowls and bigger plates on the board."
- "If you are asking about Stay and Play, include the date and number of children so we can check the details."

Bad:

- "Fuel your day the Feed way."
- "Where little ones play and grown-ups refuel."

### 2. Healthy Without The Lecture

Feed can talk about smoothies, power bowls, yoghurt, fruit, avocado, grains and fresh food. It should not talk down to people who want a burger, loaded fries or a cheesecake pot.

Use "fresh", "lighter", "filling", "easy lunch", "good coffee" and "something warm" when they fit. Avoid "clean eating", "guilt-free", "nourish your body", "wellness", "detox", "superfood" and anything that makes lunch sound like a moral decision.

### 3. Specifics Over Cafe Filler

Name real things:

- Feed Breakfast
- Veggie Feed
- breakfast until 11.30am
- hot honey chicken
- Cornish crab roll
- power bowls
- kids food
- smoothies and shakes
- Stay and Play
- Trenwith the Burrows Leisure Centre
- green panelling, wood tables, hanging bulbs and bunting

Do not lean on filler like "delicious treats", "mouth-watering options", "perfect for all occasions", "hidden gem" or "nestled in the heart of St Ives".

### 4. Plain, Useful Sentences

The reader might be checking opening times, trying to book a child into soft play, or deciding whether there is food for them after swimming. Help them quickly.

Write in complete sentences. Keep paragraphs short, but not chopped into fake drama. A sentence can be warm without being polished into a slogan.

Bad:

- "Fresh coffee. Proper food. Good vibes."
- "Your new favourite St Ives stop."

Good:

- "There is coffee, kids food, cakes and lunch on the same menu, so Stay and Play does not leave the grown-ups with nothing useful to order."
- "The board covers breakfast, lunch, sweet things and drinks, with lighter bowls next to burgers and loaded fries."

### 5. Let The Cafe Be Normal

Not every line needs to sell. Some pages just need to say what happens, where to go and what to ask for.

This is especially important for:

- Contact details
- Opening hours
- booking information
- allergy and dietary information
- Stay and Play rules
- prices
- directions

If a line affects whether someone turns up at the right place, keep it direct.

### 6. Warm, Not Saccharine

The cafe can sound friendly without calling everything "lovely", "perfect", "little", "delightful" or "wholesome". Those words get sugary quickly.

Better words:

- easy
- fresh
- warm
- filling
- useful
- lighter
- proper
- simple
- relaxed
- family-friendly

Use them only where they actually fit.

## Phrase Pool

Use sparingly. These are directions, not required phrases:

- "breakfast until 11.30am"
- "coffee and something to eat"
- "a lighter bowl"
- "something warm"
- "kids food on the board"
- "easy lunch"
- "before or after swimming"
- "close by for coffee"
- "worth checking before you come in"
- "send us the date and number of children"
- "the board changes from breakfast into lunch"

One or two per page is enough.

## No-Go List

Avoid:

- "guilt-free"
- "clean eating"
- "fuel your body"
- "wellness journey"
- "superfood-packed"
- "mouth-watering"
- "tantalising"
- "nestled"
- "hidden gem"
- "culinary destination"
- "foodie paradise"
- "perfect for all the family" repeated as filler
- "good vibes only" as a crutch, unless it is being used as actual Feed signage
- tourist-board language about St Ives
- corporate hospitality language
- over-explaining the cafe's personality

## Placeholder Copy

If information is not confirmed, write it in italics so it is easy to find later.

Examples:

- *Opening hours to confirm.*
- *Booking link, session capacity and payment flow to confirm.*
- *Parking and entrance directions to confirm.*

Do not hide missing information inside confident copy.

## Process

1. Write the useful version first.
2. Check every paragraph for vague cafe language.
3. Replace generic claims with real menu items, real details or a clear placeholder.
4. Read it aloud. If it sounds like an advert, make it more normal.
5. Check placeholders are italic before finishing.

## Where To Use It

- Homepage sections
- About page
- Menu introductions
- Stay and Play page
- Contact helper copy
- Reviews page notes
- Footer text

## Where Not To Overdo It

- Contact details
- Address and map links
- Opening hours
- Prices
- allergy information
- booking rules
- form labels
- legal text

These parts should be clear first.

## Anti-Patterns

- **Wellness waffle** - copy that makes coffee and lunch sound like self-improvement.
- **Cafe cliches** - "mouth-watering treats", "something for everyone", "hidden gem".
- **Tourism filler** - St Ives copy that could be used for any cafe near the sea.
- **Tiny slogan fragments** - "Fresh food. Good coffee. Happy kids." Use real sentences instead.
- **Too much cute family language** - the site can be family-friendly without baby-talking to parents.
- **Invented certainty** - if hours, booking rules or prices are not confirmed, use an italic placeholder.
